# Pumpkin 40055 assets and robot demo

The 52-piece layout comes from pbat's public 2013 CAD reconstruction. The inventory matches the published set inventory. Source hashes and coordinate conversions are retained in `assembly.json` and `meshes/*-sources.json`. The assembly order is inferred from the support graph and tool clearance; it is not claimed to reproduce every official instruction step. The scripted controller uses simulator body poses for feedback.

`connectors.json` resolves LDCad Shadow Library stud/socket frames at revision `9b1131fb1991f8c0bfc072325e4e12f6271aba35`. That source and the derived connector metadata are CC BY-SA 4.0; see `CONNECTOR-LICENSE.md` and https://github.com/RolandMelkert/LDCadShadowLibrary . Individual visual sources retain their license headers and attribution in their manifests. The reconstructed whole model has no explicit license declaration; part-library licenses do not imply a license for that model.

Geometry uses SI metres, column-vector transforms and WXYZ runtime quaternions. LDD coordinates are Y-up; the runtime mapping is `(x,-z,y)`. The fourteen visual designs are unchanged sourced meshes. `scripts/build_pumpkin_collisions.py` regenerates all fourteen procedural designs. The plume uses a flat blade prism and two stepped stem cylinders; its visual sculpt remains unchanged. Collision geometry is approximate: hollow rectangular shells, convex slope bodies, chamfered studs, carved insertion clearances and a simplified flat plume blade and stepped bar. Socket clearance radius is 2.70 mm and stud radius 2.35 mm. These enlarged rigid clearances substitute for material elasticity and accommodate controller tracking error; they are not calibrated LEGO fit tolerances. Tests reject colliders that fill the sockets. Convex cooking minimum thickness is explicitly 0.05 mm. Body proxies have 0.1 mm clearance per horizontal side; studs retain their original pitch. The upper plume bore has a 2.00 mm clearance radius.

`grasp-plan.json` contains initial supply poses, grasp frames and a support-first order that places small slopes before taller neighbors obstruct them. It uses prototype rigid fingertip extensions measuring 2×6×12 mm, attached as compound colliders to the existing dynamic YAM fingers. The TCP lies 156.65 mm along flange Z. Their material and inertia are not hardware-calibrated. The assembly center is 0.22 m along world X. Target IK uses a 0.1 mm-equivalent residual limit; source IK uses 3 mm. Static clearance and endpoint IK checks are planning evidence, not successful trajectories. Every production placement still requires an actual contact grasp and lift. Every source grasp receives precise TCP centering and gradual closure at 15 mm/s, lasting at least two seconds. Instantaneous closure rolled Part47 and the first bridge plate on their source supports. After lift, measured grip drift must stay below 1.5 mm and 0.05 rad before approaching the structure. Part47 uses a 30 mm approach above its low foundation: its measured tilted grasp exceeded reach on the original 70 mm overhead route.

Clutch retention is a separate prototype. Loaded insertion requires relative error below 0.8 mm and 0.05 radians, plus total upward contact with the already placed assembly above 0.15 N. The controller unloads compression and relaxes the fingers. It engages a breakable fixed joint at the measured relative pose when support contact-force magnitude is below one quarter of the joint's break-force limit. Measured-frame retention keeps the actual seated pose. The demo defaults to `PUMPKIN_CANONICAL_FOUNDATION=1`: the four foundation connections use nominal rest frames, while upper connections retain measured poses. Keeping the initial corners aligned avoids adjacent-body interference; avoiding correction on upper plates prevents fighting the still-clamped gripper. Set that option to `0` for measured frames everywhere. `PUMPKIN_CANONICAL_CLUTCH=1` uses nominal frames for every stud connection, retaining the bar at its measured insertion depth. Nominal frames let constraint forces correct the remaining small pose error after the same physical contact gates. Nominal-frame modes can visibly snap a small residual error into alignment and must not be interpreted as calibrated material behavior. Break force is 2 N per matching stud, or 0.5 N for the plume bar. These strengths and bounding-volume masses are approximations. Normal-contact reports do not include the complete contact wrench. `PUMPKIN_DEFER_CORRECTION=1` initially engages measured rest frames, waits for the actual jaw gap to reach full opening minus 1 mm, then updates selected joints to nominal rest frames and verifies the resulting pose within 0.15 mm / 0.005 rad. This self-centering clutch approximation changes constraint frames, not actor transforms; it can visibly correct pose after release. `correction-evidence.json` records the jaw gap and before/after body poses.

For placements after the first four corners, the controller measures assembly contact after every step and stops advancing when seating is detected or total support load exceeds 0.5 N. The first four use the baseline incremental insertion and check contact after each increment. Before entering neighboring brick height, the controller aligns the carried body above the current structure and descends in steps of at most 2 mm, checking lateral error below 20 micrometres and angle error below 0.0005 rad. This cancels tracking bias that otherwise exceeded the 0.20 mm gap between adjacent body proxies. Precision moves interpolate from the previous commanded pose (`start_from_command=True`), preserving that compensation throughout each move; starting again from the measured pose caused a transient sideways drift. The contact guard includes every already placed piece, including pieces that are not required parents. Each connector still requires its measured pose gate. The controller verifies finger clearance, released pose, retreat and actual PhysX joint-break events. It checks all retained connections again after both arms park. The round foundation is fixtured; the other 51 pieces remain dynamic on visible supply supports. `supply-posts.json` defines open posts fitted to each convex underside by linear programming. Tests check the full post volumes clear thin walls and sloped undersides. The plume uses an open three-wall holder; the passage clears its wider stem collar, and an end stop prevents it falling within its own plane. `scripts/plan_pumpkin_supply.py` regenerates the posts. No gripper attachments or actor pose resets occur during execution. Inactive parts may sleep; the manipulated part remains awake for force reporting.

Run `demos/demo_yam_pumpkin.py:main` with the normal simulator launcher and a fresh absolute `YAM_OUTPUT` directory. `PUMPKIN_LIMIT=1` selects the first insertion; `51` selects the full model. `PUMPKIN_GPU_PHYSICS=1` enables GPU dynamics with enlarged total and found/lost aggregate-pair buffers. Physics defaults to 240 Hz; 60 Hz caused severe supply tipping on both CPU and GPU. `PUMPKIN_PHYSICS_HZ` overrides that rate. `PUMPKIN_SETUP_TEST=1` records measured supply settling and exits without claiming assembly. `PUMPKIN_GRASP_TEST=0` tests only the plume's source grasp and explicitly records no assembly success. Add `PUMPKIN_GRASP_TRANSPORT=1` to test transport to the target pose with the target structure absent. The plume uses finger drive gains of 10,000 N/m and 100 N·s/m, capped at 5 N, to prevent the thin blade slipping during transport. These are simulator controller settings, not hardware force recommendations.

A headless validation run uses the following explicit profile (full-build validation is still in progress):

```sh
PUMPKIN_GPU_PHYSICS=1 PUMPKIN_CANONICAL_CLUTCH=1 \
PUMPKIN_CANONICAL_FOUNDATION=0 PUMPKIN_DEFER_CORRECTION=1 \
YAM_OUTPUT="$PWD/outputs/pumpkin" uv run --no-sync bricksim \
  --exp isaacsim.exp.base.python \
  --/app/window/enabled=false --/app/renderer/enabled=false --/renderer/enabled=false \
  --/exts/omni.kit.viewport.window/startup/disableWindowOnLoad=true \
  --/isaac/startup/ros_bridge_extension= demos/demo_yam_pumpkin.py:main
```

Use a fresh output directory for each trial. See [YAM setup](../../../docs/yam/README.md) for the imported arm and launcher requirements. Machine-specific NVIDIA driver workarounds are separate from the controller. `engine.json` records the actual dynamics backend, broadphase and timestep.

`python/bricksim/utils/physics_video.py` records measured rigid-body poses, meshes and per-body contact pairs for the carried piece and previously placed pieces. Run `uv run --no-project scripts/render_physics_video.py RUN_DIRECTORY` for a CPU-rendered replay when native RTX capture is unavailable. Replays use recorded poses without target-pose animation or interpolation. Failed and partial trials remain labeled as such.

Validation is in progress. The longest full run completed 14 robot placements before a bridge insertion collided with a neighboring piece. In a separate diagnostic, 14 pieces were initialized at their assembled poses and the corrected controller completed that one insertion, including release, retreat and parked-arm checks, in 184 seconds (196 seconds including startup). Those initialized pieces are not robot placements. A matched diagnostic with the previous descent broke four connections; preserving compensated commands removed the transient sideways drift in the passing test. The full sequence is being rerun from loose supplies.

The plume has passed a contact grasp, 69.8 mm lift and transport to the empty target pose with 0.76 mm / 0.00238 rad error. This does not verify insertion into the upper cone. No complete 51-placement pumpkin run has passed yet; a partial replay must not be presented as a completed assembly.
